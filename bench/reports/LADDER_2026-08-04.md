# The ladder: two rungs, five arms, and a reproducibility gradient

**2026-08-04, the primary node, on the 5090 while both live seats stayed on the 4090.**
Instrument: `setlist.py`, one model at a time, one call in flight, temperature 0,
two passes per record. Anchors: `reid_dictionary_v2` (digest `54961ca5a018feaa`)
and the operations sample (digest `cf6189527c3c3f86`). Floor: DMF. Dated
observation, labeled inference.

## What had to be rebuilt first

`reid_dictionary_v1` could not answer any question asked of it. Four of twelve
anchors were undecoded PDF, and nine of twelve returned an empty field list from
every model, so roughly two anchors discriminated. The cause was the selection
rule: it stratified on a grade a 120B assigned by reading the WHOLE document,
then truncated the page to 6,000 characters, and nothing checked that the graded
content survived the cut.

v2 fixes exactly that, and the rejection counts show how large the problem was:

| rejection reason | pages |
|---|---:|
| graded content did not survive the truncation window | **1,992** |
| declared negative but contains reference fields | 51 |
| undecoded bytes | 43 |
| too short | 135 |

Every v2 positive is PROVEN to retain at least four reference fields inside the
window, and every negative is proven to retain none. Selection never calls a
model, so the set cannot be biased toward whichever model probes it; it uses
recorded text properties plus `fields_observed`, the checklist a 120B produced at
stage 4. That checklist is a REFERENCE and not ground truth, and it buys
something v1 could not support at all: recall.

## Four findings

**1. The QAT republish is inert, now on a fourth independent instrument.** 12 of
12 identical on a different anchor set, on a harder task, at a different token
budget. Combined with 120 of 120 on the operations rung overnight, the 2026-07-17
"corrected vocabulary" commit changes nothing this fabric can measure. The build decision on the primary carrier is settled on evidence rather than
on preference.

**2. The owed confirming step is closed, and the two rungs disagree in a way
that resolves it.** The 2026-08-02 casting amendment said the carriers are on the
wrong quant, named UD-Q4_K_XL, and required "the UD build on one QAT node, the
frozen anchor set, one variable" before calling it a fix. That is now run.

UD is genuinely different from QAT: 2 of 12 identical, Jaccard 0.598. It is
**markedly more self-consistent**, 50 percent exact against 16 and Jaccard 0.751
against 0.615, which is consistent with the amendment's case that QAT produces
unstable output. But its **recall is lower**, 0.489 against 0.572, on
indistinguishable latency.

Reading both rungs together is what makes this decidable, and it is why a ladder
beats a single measurement:

| | bounded rung | open rung |
|---|---:|---:|
| QAT self-consistency | 98% exact | 0.615 Jaccard |
| UD self-consistency | 97% exact | **0.751 Jaccard** |
| QAT against UD | 72% identical | 0.598 Jaccard |

**UD is never meaningfully worse and is substantially better exactly where
reproducibility is scarce.** On the bounded work the carriers actually do, the
two are a tie at 97 against 98 percent with indistinguishable latency. On open
extraction, where QAT falls to 0.615, UD holds 0.751. The recall cost sits only
on the rung where the carriers are not the right seat anyway.

**Recommendation, Tier 3, for the operator's ruling: swap the carriers to
UD-Q4_K_XL.** The amendment's premise is confirmed, the risk is bounded by a tie
on their actual workload, and the gain lands on the failure mode the amendment
named. The one honest caveat is that recall was measured on twelve anchors, so
the 0.489 against 0.572 is directional rather than settled.

**3. Reproducibility is task-dependent, sharply, and that is the ladder's real
product.** Same model, same temperature 0, same instrument, two rungs:

| rung | self-Jaccard, E4B QAT |
|---|---:|
| bounded schema-fill, ~200 characters | **0.98** |
| open field extraction, 6,000 characters | **0.615** |

The routing law's rule that a single read is not a measurement was earned on long
judgment prompts and confirmed yesterday to relax for bounded ones. It does NOT
relax for open extraction. A single read is trustworthy at one end of this ladder
and not at the other, and the boundary sits between two tasks that both look like
"extraction" from the outside.

**4. The 26B cannot do this task inside the fleet's context budget.** 41 percent
parse, 31 second median, 23 fields found against the 4B's 236, and recall of
0.086. Its perfect self-consistency is again the artifact of returning almost
nothing. This is not a clean quality comparison: at 6,000 max_tokens on a 6,000
character page inside an 8,192 context, it runs out of room to reason. That is
itself the finding. On the fleet as configured, this model is not a candidate
for open extraction at this page size, and no amount of prompt work changes the
arithmetic of the context window.

## What the broken anchors had been hiding

The v1 set reported the 26B at 3.48s median and the E4B at 2.41s, a 1.4x gap. On
v2 the same two arms read 31.08s and 4.33s, a 7.2x gap. The contaminated set was
not merely noisy, it was systematically flattering the larger model, because
most of its anchors had nothing to extract and returning an empty list is fast.

That is worth carrying forward as a rule rather than an anecdote: **an instrument
whose items do not discriminate will report that everything is similar.** It will
not look broken. It will look like a null result.
