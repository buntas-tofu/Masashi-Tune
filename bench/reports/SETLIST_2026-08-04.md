# The first setlist: what four arms said about two republishes

**2026-08-03, 23:13 to 23:51 on the primary node.** Phase 1 of the overnight. Four phases,
960 calls, zero errors. Instrument: `setlist.py`, one model at a time on the
5090 while the 4090 carried only its two live seats. Workload: 120 operations
records frozen at digest `cf6189527c3c3f86`, balanced 60/60 on whether the
record asserts dates. Temperature 0, two passes per record, one call in flight.
Floor: DMF. Dated observation, labeled inference.

## Three findings, and the second one reverses an assumption

**1. The E4B republish changed nothing that can be measured.** The two builds
are different files: 2,144 bytes apart in size, with different sha256 over the
first 64MB, which is where a GGUF keeps its metadata and vocabulary. They
produced byte-identical normalised output on all 120 records. The
corrected-vocabulary hypothesis is now dead twice over, once on the extraction
anchors this afternoon where parse did not move off 67 percent, and once here at
ten times the sample with a paired design.

**2. The 26B republish DID change something, which the E4B result had implied it
would not.** Seventeen of 120 records differ between the stale and corrected 26B
builds, and the corrected build is better on the axis that matters for
scheduling: p95 falls from 5.57s to 4.01s, a 28 percent improvement at the tail,
with the median improving 7 percent. So the face flip earlier on 2026-08-03 was
not the cosmetic currency update the E4B result would have suggested. It was a
real behavioural change and it went the right way. Inference, Tier 3: whatever
unsloth corrected between `b68961b3` and `c099eb48` reached the weights, whereas
whatever Google corrected for E4B did not reach anything this workload touches.

**3. A bounded prompt at temperature 0 is reproducible, and that is the big
one.** `extraction_bench.py` named this as its open question: a seat overlapped
its own extracted field list by 0.311 on a 20k-token judgment prompt, and the
extraction work found only 28 percent of extracted sets identical between two
reads at a seat's default sampler. The answer for a SHORT bounded prompt at
temperature 0 is 98 to 99 percent self-consistent across all four arms. That is
not a marginal improvement, it is a different regime, and it means a single read
CAN be trusted for bounded extraction where it demonstrably cannot be for long
judgment prompts. The routing law's warning that a single read is not a
measurement is intact and remains correct where it was earned; what is now
established is where its blast radius ends.

## The routing law, confirmed on a clean paired comparison

The law says bounded schema-filling goes to the small fast seats and that fit is
empirical rather than inferred from parameter count. On identical records:

- E4B, 4B active: 100% parse, 1.98s median, 2.53s p95
- 26B A4B: 99% parse, 2.46s median, 4.01s p95

The 4B is faster, tighter at the tail, and marginally better at producing valid
JSON. Six and a half times the parameters bought nothing here and cost 24 percent
on the median and 58 percent at p95. The 26B also hit the 1500-token ceiling on
a handful of calls where E4B never did, because it reasons longer before it
answers, which is the same trait that makes it the better seat for work this
bench does not measure.

## What this says about the primary carrier

The open decision was whether the primary carrier keeps the corrected E4B build or reverts
to match the hub, specialist and AMD carriers. On the evidence the choice is free: the builds
are behaviourally identical, so no quality argument favours either.

That leaves the tiebreaker to two standing positions, and they agree.
Uniform substrate is the top value, and the upgrade-forward posture says stay
current for business reasons rather than nostalgia. Rolling the corrected build
to all four carriers satisfies both at once and costs nothing measurable, where
reverting the primary carrier satisfies uniformity by moving away from upstream.

**Recommendation: roll the corrected E4B build to the hub, specialist and AMD carriers
rather than reverting the primary carrier.** Operator ruling, not an agent decision.

## Method notes worth keeping

- The setlist ran on the 5090 because both primary-node seats are pinned to the 4090.
  The runner asserts which physical GPU each phase landed on and refuses to
  measure on the wrong one. That check exists because the first launch failed it:
  nvidia-smi enumerates by PCI bus, CUDA defaults to FASTEST_FIRST, and the bench
  set `CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES` without `CUDA_DEVICE_ORDER`. The aborted rows are
  kept under `runs/_voided`.
- A setlist varies only the weights, so this doubles as the calibration harness
  the routing law requires before any stage may be split across seats for
  throughput. The offsets above are that artifact for these four models.
- Phase load cost, which is the number a setlist needs to schedule a phase: 2s
  for a 4.8G model, 12s for a 15.8G one, unload effectively instant.
